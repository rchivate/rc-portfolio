A Comparative Analysis of Match Outcomes for India and Pakistan in Asia Cup History

Problem Definition

Historically, the rivalry between the Indian and the Pakistani cricket teams has always been prevalent. This competition is also highlighted throughout the Asia Cup. Asia Cup is a tournament that includes several teams that represent different countries in the continent of Asia.

One of the most important actions that a captain of a cricket team makes is the coin toss. The coin toss can help set up the framework for the match. If won, the captain is able to make the decision of batting or bowling first. This decision is strategically important based on the strengths and weaknesses of the two teams. Usually the tension between the two teams starts here.

This project aims to determine: To what extent does winning the toss influence the final match outcome for the two historically rival teams: India and Pakistan? Furthermore, I explore whether the decision made (voluntarily or involuntarily) has a visible impact on the final outcome of the match.

This relationship between the decision made after the coin toss and the result of the match provides fans and sports analysts with data to back up claims. These claims can range from whether the coin toss, associated with luck, is a determining factor in the outcome of the specific match, and how significant the coin toss is to the outcome.

Because India and Pakistan are historically rivals when it comes to cricket, it is interesting to understand if something as trivial as a coin flip can set up the team for success or failure. The analysis of historical data can also form the basis for claims that fans make about team performance.

Data Description

This dataset from Kaggle contains data about each individual match played in the Asia Cup between 1984 and 2022.

Each row of the dataset represents an individual match. Each team gets its own row, meaning there are duplicates for matches. The difference between the two rows is the perspective. For example, in a game between India and Pakistan, one row reflects India’s statistics (opponent: Pakistan) and the other reflects Pakistan’s statistics (opponent: India).

The key columns used in this research are Team, Toss, Selection, and Result. These are essential because they directly address the relationship between winning the toss, the post-toss decision, and the final match outcome.

The dataset contains 250 rows and 20 columns, which can be interpreted as 250 team-match entries with 20 data points per entry. Due to the dataset’s size and scope, the analysis focuses only on India and Pakistan.

After filtering, India had 57 rows (matches) and Pakistan had 54 rows (matches). Since these numbers are relatively close, there is no significant imbalance in sample size between the two teams.

While the dataset provides detailed match information, additional data such as time of day, team rankings, and dew factor could improve accuracy. Dew can influence ball behavior and significantly impact bowling performance.

The dataset also assumes fair weather conditions and consistent rules across years. However, match conditions and regulations may vary, potentially influencing outcomes.

Data Cleaning & Preparation

Several cleaning steps were performed to ensure clarity and consistency.

  • Standardized the Result and Toss columns to lowercase.
  • Converted “win” and “lose” values to 1 and 0 for numerical analysis.
  • Filtered out “no result” and null rows.
  • Created separate datasets for India and Pakistan.
  • Grouped “win” and “win d/l” together for consistency.

Using numerical values allowed calculation of the mean, where a value above 0.5 indicates a majority winning percentage under a specific condition.

Data Understanding and Visualization

India

The bar plot for India shows a higher percentage of wins when the team wins the toss. A mean greater than 0.5 indicates a strong winning tendency under that condition.

Pakistan

Pakistan’s analysis shows that the toss does not significantly affect overall outcomes, with only a 0.4 difference observed. This suggests that Pakistan’s performance is less influenced by toss outcomes compared to India.

Heatmaps and bar charts illustrate the relationship between toss outcome, selection choice, and win percentage. For India, winning the toss clearly increases winning probability. Pakistan does not display as strong a pattern.

In both teams, bowling first is associated with slightly higher win percentages compared to batting first.

Storytelling & Interpretation

The data reveals an intriguing interplay between strategy and luck. Winning the toss significantly increases India's chances of winning, suggesting strong strategic utilization of the advantage. Pakistan’s outcomes appear less dependent on toss results, indicating performance may rely more on in-game execution.

However, correlation does not imply causation. Winning the toss does not guarantee victory. Opponent strength and match context also influence outcomes.

Limitations, Ethics, & Reflection

  • Lack of environmental factors (dew, pitch type).
  • No team rankings or player availability data.
  • Limited only to Asia Cup matches.
  • Potential misuse in sports betting contexts.

If extended, future work could integrate weather data to evaluate time-of-day effects on toss advantage.
